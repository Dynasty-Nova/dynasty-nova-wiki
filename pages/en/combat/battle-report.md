# Reading a battle report

> **In short**: a battle report gives, for each side, the units engaged, lost and surviving, then the outcome, the loot and the debris field created. Every battle can be replayed identically.
{.is-info}

## Rules

1. For **each side**, the report lists units **engaged**, **lost** and **surviving**.
2. The **outcome** is one of three: attacker victory, defender victory, draw.
3. On attacker victory, the report shows the **loot** taken (metal, crystal, hydrogen).
4. It also shows the **debris field** created (metal and crystal).
5. **Partial view**: you only see the enemy units actually engaged in the battle.
6. If your **Repair Station** recovered ships, they appear among losses, with a note: they will come back once repaired.
7. If a **moon** forms from the debris, the report says so.
8. Every battle is **deterministic**: it can be replayed and will give exactly the same result.
9. The report can be **shared** with your alliance.

## Worked example

You attack with 20 Assailants and 5 Stellar Freighters a planet defended by 10 Ballistic Projectors. The report shows:
- Attacker: 25 engaged, 3 Assailants lost, 22 surviving.
- Defender: 10 Projectors engaged, 10 lost. Attacker victory.
- Debris field: 30% of 3 × (6,000 metal + 4,000 crystal), so **5,400 metal and 3,600 crystal**. Destroyed Projectors leave no debris.
- On the defender's side, each destroyed Projector then has a 70% chance of being repaired.

## Common pitfalls

- **Losses shown may come back**: ships recovered by the Repair Station are counted among losses.
- **So may lost defenses**: 70% come back on average after the battle.
- **A draw brings nothing**: no loot.
